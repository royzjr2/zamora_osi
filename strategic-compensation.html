Strategic Compensation & Measures of Excellence

Unlike the rest of this framework, this system is already built and already running. It is in its data capture year, which is the window where design intent and implementation either stay aligned or quietly separate.

Irving has replaced tenure-based principal pay with compensation tied to demonstrated effectiveness, structured around a full data capture year before any dollars move, and sequenced so that leadership submits to the instrument first. The architecture is sound. The open question in a system this new is never the design. It is whether implementation still matches what the design assumed.

I. The system and its design intent

Beginning with 2026–27, a principal's base salary is determined by an Effectiveness Level rather than years of service. The 2025–26 year runs as a transition and data capture year: every principal receives an effectiveness rating on the new instrument, but compensation continues under the existing structure while the data is collected and validated.

T-PESS remains the official appraisal instrument. What is new is a compensation model that treats T-PESS as one component of a broader effectiveness picture rather than the whole of it, a deliberate choice, since an appraisal instrument alone was never built to carry compensation weight.

Systems like this carry a layer that never makes it into the guidebook: why each element was constructed the way it was. Which weights were deliberate, which thresholds were calibrated against what, which provisions exist because someone anticipated a specific failure. That reasoning is the first thing lost when a system changes hands and the hardest thing to reconstruct afterward, and recovering it is usually more valuable than redesigning around its absence.

The design choice worth noticing

The rollout sequence is principals first, then assistant principals, then teachers. The internal framing is modeling what we expect by going first. Leadership submits to the instrument before anyone else is asked to. That sequencing was intentional and it is load-bearing. It is also the principle that should govern any new measurement introduced anywhere in this district, including at central office.

Component measures shift by campus type so that early childhood, elementary, middle, and high school leaders are evaluated on what is actually measurable in their setting. Each level maps to a salary band, and bands differ across Special Programs, Elementary, Middle and Singley, and High School assignments. A campus receiving a D or F state accountability rating caps its principal's evaluation score at the top of the Progressing range, regardless of points earned elsewhere.

III. Three scoring approaches

Different kinds of measures need different scoring logic, and the system uses three deliberately.

ApproachHow it worksApplied to
ProportionalRaw score converted to a percentage of possible, multiplied by component weightT-PESS, STAAR Domain 3, LIT rubric
Tiered achievementPerformance bands set in standard deviations above district average, using local rather than national normsNWEA MAP growth, Circle growth
Target plus stretchBase points for meeting a defined goal, additional points for exceeding itAttendance, TSI, Circle performance, family engagement

Tiered bands are built from Irving's own distribution rather than national norms. That was a deliberate decision: principals are measured against what is achievable in this district's context rather than a benchmark set elsewhere. It also means the bands are only valid as long as the underlying distribution is recalculated. A national norm holds still, a local one does not.

The most interesting measure in the system

Teacher Effectiveness is scored on the correlation between a principal's T-TESS ratings and NWEA MAP growth. It does not ask whether teachers performed well. It asks whether the principal's ratings actually distinguish stronger teachers from weaker ones. The purpose is specific: a compensation system that will eventually extend to teachers depends entirely on appraisal ratings meaning something, and this measure puts a price on rating validity before that extension happens.

IV. Stability by design

The implementation rules are conservative in ways that matter for trust.

2025–26
Data capture
Ratings issued on the new instrument. Pay continues under the existing structure. Nothing is at stake financially.
2026–27
Implementation year 1
Hold harmless. No principal earns less than their prior schedule amount. Increases available, decreases blocked.
2027–28
Two-year average begins
Effectiveness Level set by rolling average of the two most recent ratings rather than a single year.
Ongoing
One level per year
Movement capped at a single Effectiveness Level in either direction annually, regardless of what the average indicates.

Hold harmless in year one, a rolling two-year average from year three, and a one-level movement cap together mean no principal experiences a sudden financial shock from a single difficult year. Provisions for extended leave and late hires void ratings rather than penalizing incomplete years.

That conservatism has a cost worth naming: a genuinely improving principal also climbs slowly, and a principal placed low in the first cycle takes several years to reach a band their performance would otherwise justify. Whether that trade is right is a values question, not a technical one, but it should be a conscious answer rather than an emergent one.

V. What needs verification

Systems built by one group and implemented by another develop gaps. Not through carelessness, but through the ordinary process of people making reasonable local decisions without access to the reasoning behind the original design. A data capture year is exactly when those gaps should be found, because nothing is financially at stake yet.

These are the places worth looking first, ordered by how expensive they become if left unexamined.

Do the current business rules match the documented ones?

The first verification is the simplest and the most important: pull the calculation logic actually in use and reconcile it line by line against the guidebook. Where they differ, the question is not which is right but which was intended, and whether the change was a deliberate improvement or an undocumented workaround that has since become permanent.

Have the tiered bands been recalculated against current district data?

Standard deviation bands built from Irving's own distribution are only valid while that distribution is current. If the bands were set once and carried forward, they are measuring principals against a district that no longer exists. This is the most likely place for silent drift and one of the easiest to correct.

Cut points are reviewed annually. On what basis, and by whom?

The guidebook states cut points are evaluated annually to remain accurate and appropriately challenging. That is correct practice and also the most contestable element in the system. Without a documented methodology covering what evidence triggers a change, who decides, and how it is communicated, an annual judgment call becomes an annual argument.

What does the score distribution actually look like?

If principals cluster into one or two Effectiveness Levels, the instrument is not discriminating and the compensation differentiation it exists to produce will not materialize. This is the single question the data capture year was designed to answer, and the answer should be examined before implementation rather than after.

Culture and climate scores participation, not experience

Five points awarded on survey completion rate. That was a deliberate first-cycle choice: establish participation before scoring results, because scoring results too early suppresses honest responses. It was always intended as a stepping stone, and the listening architecture is what completes it.

Is the audit trail sufficient to reconstruct a single principal's score?

Business rules, data sources, validation steps, calculation logic, and timelines should be documented well enough that any individual result can be rebuilt a year later by someone who was not in the room. Compensation systems are litigated in hallways long before anywhere else, and documentation is the entire defense.

VI. How it connects to everything else

Strategic Compensation is not a standalone program, and its placement in this portfolio is what makes the rest of the framework coherent.

To campus capacity. Principal tenure is a direct component of the campus capacity measure. A compensation system built to retain effective principals should show up as rising leadership stability over time. That is a testable hypothesis, and the panel data exists to test it.

To organizational health. The culture and climate component currently measures participation. The listening architecture is what would eventually let it measure experience, and the two systems are already touching through the same surveys.

To portfolio and capacity work. Reconfiguration changes campus classifications, which changes salary bands. Any consolidation decision has direct compensation consequences for the principals involved, and those need to be modeled before decisions are announced rather than discovered afterward.

To the effectiveness model. The sequencing logic here, leadership goes first and then each next layer, is exactly the principle any central office measurement should follow. Principals have already accepted an instrument that determines their pay. That is a reasonable argument that central office should accept one too.

The honest summary

The architecture is sound and the sequencing is right. What a system like this needs at this stage is not redesign but verification: confirming that what runs matches what was designed, recalculating what was always meant to be recalculated, and documenting it to the standard compensation requires. Then connecting it to the capacity, climate, and portfolio work so it stops operating as a standalone program.

A compensation system earns its authority the same way an appraisal does, by being explainable to the person it is applied to.
