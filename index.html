Place accountable for how it all fits together

Districts rarely fail for lack of information. They fail because no one is accountable for how the information fits together: where enrollment is heading, whether capacity matches it, whether staffing follows, whether compensation rewards what we say matters, and whether any of it is being implemented as designed.

That means one place reconciles the enrollment number, the staffing model, and the compensation design against each other before they reach the Board, not after they have already drifted apart.

The system

Six domains that have to arrive together

Each of these already exists somewhere in the district. What does not exist is the layer where they get reconciled against each other before anything reaches the Board.

Leadership capacity is an output here rather than a seventh domain, because its inputs are already distributed across the six. Campus capacity measurement tracks principal tenure and flags where leadership stability is eroding. Strategic compensation holds T-PESS results and effectiveness levels. Organizational health carries the administrator feedback instruments and competency frameworks. Portfolio work forces placement decisions. Planning treats succession as a continuity risk.

Assembled, those produce something no single division can see on its own: an honest picture of who is ready, where the bench is thin, and which campuses are about to lose the leadership their results depend on. That is a synthesis problem, which is why it belongs to the layer rather than to any one domain.

Sequence

The first twelve months

Order matters more than speed. Several of these actions are only viable in a specific relation to the others, and publishing before the numbers are defensible costs more credibility than moving slowly.

Days 1–90

Listen, inventory, establish truth

Meet with executive leadership and inventory the diagnostics and data that already exist. Reconcile enrollment and capacity figures. Understand where Strategic Compensation is working and where trust is thin. Publish nothing until the numbers hold.

Months 4–8

Build the instruments

Stand up the first organizational health diagnostic. Draft executive scorecards with the Superintendent and Cabinet. Establish the enterprise review cadence. Define the leadership competency framework. Bring the first capacity and enrollment picture to the table.

Months 9–12

Make it consequential

First full review cycle completed. First scorecard reported to the Board. Strategic Compensation cycle executed against documented, auditable rules. Recommendations delivered on what to continue, refine, expand, or stop, with evidence behind each.

Operating posture

How this function operates

Lead through influence

Division leaders retain operational ownership. The leverage here is clarity, evidence, and follow-through, not authority over their teams.

Publish on a calendar

Scorecards, reviews, and reports on fixed dates. Predictability is what makes measurement feel like governance rather than surveillance.

Say the inconvenient thing

The value of this function is unfiltered judgment. An advisor who only confirms what is already believed is not worth the position.

Close every loop

Nobody answers a second survey if the first one changed nothing. Visible response is the precondition for honest data.
